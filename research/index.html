Shenton Park Observatory (MPC D21) has contributed to astronomical research since 2001, including supernova discovery, minor planet recovery and follow-up, variable-star photometry, and spectroscopy.

This page brings together authored or co-authored publications, discoveries, published observations and database contributions. Search ADS for publications by Luckas, P.

Minor planet discoveries (2007–2010)

(391496) Colineldridge (2007 PG9) – a main-belt asteroid discovered at D21 Shenton Park on 11 August 2007, subsequently named in honour of Western Australian astronomy educator Colin Eldridge.

2009 TV – a Mars-crossing asteroid discovered at D21 Shenton Park on 8 October 2009. Its perihelion distance is approximately 1.52 AU, with an orbital eccentricity of 0.414.

(316258) 2010 OE100 – a main-belt asteroid discovered at D21 Shenton Park on 20 July 2010. Its perihelion distance is approximately 2.11 AU, with an orbital eccentricity of 0.188.

NEO close-approach follow-up

Photometric database contributions

As of October 2017, 1,420  observations of variable stars have been submitted to the AAVSO from photometry acquired at SPO. These can be found searching aavso.org using observatory code LPAE.

Spectroscopic database contributions

Spectra acquired at Shenton Park Observatory contribute to the ARAS and BeSS databases, supporting professional and amateur studies of stellar behaviour. These database contributions provide a record of the observatory’s spectroscopic work.

ARAS submissions (2016–2026)

The ARAS spectral database brings together observations of novae, symbiotic stars and other variable stars. As of November 2026, it lists 164 spectra of 41 targets credited to Shenton Park Observatory (observer code PJL), observed from 20 October 2016 to 9 November 2026.
