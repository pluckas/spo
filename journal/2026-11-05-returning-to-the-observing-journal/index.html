Recommissioning the website

05 November 2026 · Observatory updates

It has been more than eight years since the last entry here. The observatory has remained active during that time, but keeping the website up to date has received less attention.

I’m now bringing the site back into use, mainly to preserve the archive. The earlier posts record observing projects, equipment changes, software development and experiments stretching back many years. Some are still useful references; others are simply reminders of what I was working on at the time. I’d like to keep that record accessible.

The site has been simplified, with published observations and results collected separately from the journal and image archives. Older entries retain their original dates and describe the equipment, methods and circumstances of their time.

The observatory today

The main instrument remains the RC14C Ritchey-Chrétien telescope, now mounted on a PlaneWave L-350 and used with a Shelyak eShel spectrograph. Work in recent times has focused increasingly on automation, including target acquisition, guiding, calibration and target observing sequences.

Spectro Capture1 has been a substantial part of that work, bringing these steps together for unattended observing.

The spectroscopy itself has also continued to support collaborative research, with observations contributed to shared databases such as ARAS, BeSS and a small number of direct pro-am collaborations.

The observatory history page now includes the current setup alongside photographs of earlier equipment and modifications.

Record keeping

There is no plan to reconstruct every development since the last post or return to regular blogging. The main purpose is to retain the material already here as a personal record of the observatory and its work.

I may add posts when there is something significant to document—a change to the instrument, a useful piece of software, an observing project or a result worth recording. For now, the priority is preserving the archive and making it easier to find things again.

1 Luckas, P. (2026). “Spectro Capture: A software system for automated small-observatory spectroscopy.” Astronomy and Computing, 57, 101162. doi:10.1016/j.ascom.2026.101162.
